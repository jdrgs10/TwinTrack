## SAFE TO SAY

- “TwinTrack V2 is an evidence-informed experimental seven-input model trained on synthetic data. Its real-student predictive accuracy remains unverified.”
- “In our completed NetHealth comparison, previous academic performance was the strongest predictor. Adding early-semester wearable measures did not convincingly improve prediction.” This is about that cohort and design; see [the synthesis](FINAL_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md#2-frozen-nethealth-result-and-consistency-review).
- “NetHealth included 207 students and 963 repeated student-semester observations; validation separated students.”
- “The HSLS variable/access audit and local preanalysis protocol are prepared. Its participant-level comparison is pending.”
- “MCS is planning only; COMPASS is a researched candidate. Neither has a completed TwinTrack participant-level analysis.”
- “The experimental personal RHR wellbeing trend is separate from the academic model and does not diagnose illness or change the prediction.”

## MUST QUALIFY

- **“Lifestyle effects are small.”** Say the completed NetHealth wearable block produced no convincing incremental prediction, with uncertainty and selection limitations. It did not test study/screen time or prove lifestyles universally irrelevant.
- **“Prior grades are strongest.”** Established within NetHealth; dominant in synthetic V2 by design. Not a demonstrated universal finding across completed HSLS/MCS/COMPASS comparisons.
- **“What-If shows an improvement.”** It shows the frozen model's numerical sensitivity, not a causal or promised score gain.
- **“V2 outperforms V1.”** Existing comparisons use synthetic profiles under specified assumptions, not prospective student outcomes.
- **“V2 R² is approximately 0.58 / MAE approximately 6.53.”** These are held-out **synthetic** evaluation values; not real-world accuracy or individual confidence bounds.
- **“Research supports sleep.”** Relevant observational evidence concerns particular populations/windows and does not establish TwinTrack's latest-window slope or causal benefit. [Okano et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-019-0055-z).
- **“COMPASS supports restrained activity effects.”** This refers to an external published association study, not a TwinTrack experiment or seven-input validation. [Papasideris et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253142).
- **“HSLS is preregistered.”** It has a dated local preanalysis freeze and checksum; no external registry or independent timestamp. Lawful school-group access is still needed.
- **“Research is sufficient.”** Sufficient for an honestly labelled experimental graduation prototype, subject to the actual rubric; not sufficient to claim validated real-student accuracy or clinical usefulness.

## DO NOT SAY

- “TwinTrack predicts real students accurately,” “validated for UAE teenagers,” or “reliable for important academic decisions.”
- “NetHealth validates TwinTrack V2,” “HSLS validates the seven-input model,” or “all four datasets confirmed our model.”
- “HSLS/MCS/COMPASS comparisons are complete,” or “HSLS found no effect” while its result remains pending.
- “More sleep/study causes better grades,” “less screen time guarantees a score increase,” or “the Ridge slope is a measured causal effect.”
- “More steps reduce grades,” “exercise is bad for achievement,” or “lowering RHR raises your mark.”
- “Average daily HR in NetHealth is the same as resting HR.”
- “All seven inputs are scientifically necessary,” or “every feature has equally strong evidence.”
- “Synthetic evaluation, golden parity or passing tests establishes real-world accuracy.”
- “963 independent NetHealth students,” “less than 0.1% change on every metric,” or a pooled confidence interval presented as if it estimated a different fold-average statistic.
- “The RHR trend diagnoses illness, detects emergencies, or uses clinically validated teenage thresholds.”
