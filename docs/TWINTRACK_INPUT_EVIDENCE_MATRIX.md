# TwinTrack: seven-input academic-evidence matrix

Research cutoff: **6 October 2026**. This matrix accompanies the [scientific foundation](FINAL_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md); it is a critical review, not a formal GRADE assessment or predictive validation. Ratings concern the named input and academic outcomes. They do not rate general health benefits, sensor accuracy, or TwinTrack's model performance. Effect estimates below retain their original outcomes, scales, and designs; they are **not a pooled range, transferable dose, or TwinTrack coefficient**. Unless otherwise indicated, intervals are 95% confidence intervals.

**Rating definitions:** **STRONG SUPPORT** means consistent, directly relevant evidence for an academic relationship; **MODERATE SUPPORT** means reasonably consistent evidence with important design or applicability limits; **MIXED / INCONSISTENT** means meaningful positive, null, opposing, or context-dependent findings; **WEAK DIRECT SUPPORT** means sparse or indirect evidence for the exact input despite adjacent literature; **INSUFFICIENT DIRECT EVIDENCE** means the available direct studies cannot establish a dependable relationship or the specified predictive claim. The final category does not mean no studies exist.

## Evidence notes and strongest limitations

**Previous score.** Rimfeld et al. followed approximately 6,000 UK twin pairs across ages 7–16; the correlations in the matrix describe attainment stability, not immutability ([2018, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0030-0)). Simonsmeier et al.'s 493-study synthesis included 126,050 participants: prior/post knowledge r≈.53, CI .509–.552, I²=94.16%, but normalized gain r=−.059 ([2022, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2021.1939700)). That level-versus-gain distinction is the strongest qualification: a predictor of later level need not predict improvement. Subject and assessment comparability matter; noisy scores and ceiling effects can distort apparent change. Neither paper validates a particular 1–90-day lookback or deployed coefficient.

**Study time.** Guo et al. reviewed 11 publications; 10 supplied 14 reports for homework-versus-none g=.45 [.24,.66], I²=71.30%; dose comparisons were too sparse to pool, and the two RCTs had high bias risk ([2024, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cl2.1431)). In Spanish adolescents, Fernández-Alonso et al. analyzed 7,451 students from 7,725 recruited; autonomy and assignment frequency mattered more than raw time ([2015, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000032); abstract/indexed excerpts). Agarwal et al.'s 50 classroom experiments support retrieval practice, a strategy rather than duration ([2021, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9)). Minutes may increase because material is hard or understanding is weak. An apparent optimum is therefore not a prescription, and strategy evidence cannot establish a minutes-only feature's causal slope.

**Sleep duration.** Musshafen et al.'s US secondary-school synthesis included 14 studies, 11 meta-analyzed; duration r=.030 [−.027,.087] was nonsignificant ([2021, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleep.2021.04.015); abstract/excerpts). A newer broad child/adolescent synthesis reported duration r=.132 [.099,.164], while its overall sleep construct had I²=96.87%; its 59 articles and 163,357 total participants are not a duration-subgroup N ([2026, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16050634); indexed excerpts). Evanger et al.'s 1,092 Norwegian students had a positive two-year association with registry grades after prior-GPA adjustment, B=.007 scale-points/minute [.003,.012] ([2025, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.14430)). [Cousins et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jadohealth.2019.04.030) supply experimental adolescent factual-memory evidence under severe restriction, not ordinary grade forecasting. These studies do not supply an individual dose or validate yesterday's sleep. The near-zero secondary-school synthesis is substantive counterevidence; mood, illness, exam load, and reverse direction remain possible, unproven influences.

**Recreational screens.** Adelantado-Renau et al. reviewed 58 studies and meta-analyzed 30 (106,653 participants; 480,479 across all 58): aggregate d=−.29 [−.65,.08], I²=96.4%; adolescent TV/gaming associations were negative ([2019, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2019.3176)). In Sweden, weekday ≥5 versus ≤2 hours at age 13 predicted lower odds of stronger grades at 16: OR=.401, **99%** CI .199–.810; weekend use was nonsignificant ([2025, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/apa.70024)). Contradictory Saudi evidence linked higher attainment to greater odds of >2-hour leisure use, OR=2.25 [1.48,3.43], with screen exposure modeled as outcome ([2021, DOI](https://doi.org/10.15537/smj.2021.42.8.20210310)). Do not reverse that OR into screen benefits. Activity, timing, and thresholds matter; none establishes a universal linear penalty.

**Exercise minutes.** The 92-RCT review by Vasilopoulos et al. covered ages 5–12; the total 25,334 participants also include cognitive outcomes, not solely academic ones. Overall academic g=.14 [−.04,.31] was nonsignificant ([2023, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09760-2)). Norwegian School in Motion randomized schools, enrolling 2,084 age-14 students; numeracy benefits were small, and one academically successful arm had no measured PA effect ([2021, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-021-10901-x)). A separate 599-student Norwegian longitudinal cohort found no GPA association with objective activity changes ([2021, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pmedr.2021.101312)). The combination argues against importing a school-program effect into an Apple-minute coefficient. Programs also change instruction and social experience. Exercise Time is a credited-intensity measure under [Apple's definition](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkactivitysummary/appleexercisetime), not direct measurement of fitness or a research-defined MVPA dose.

**Steps.** Li et al. studied 5,515 adolescents, mean age 11.93, using three-week Fitbit steps and reported grades one year later; the main continuous academic association was null ([1 October 2026, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.36973)). An exploratory category does not establish a threshold; the main numerical coefficient/CI was unavailable. Shore et al.'s six-week middle-school program involved 46 intervention and 46 control students: steps increased without a differential academic benefit ([2014, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1054773813485240); abstract-only). These are direct contrary findings, not proof every possible association is exactly zero. A single prior-day count cannot be presumed equivalent to habitual multiweek activity; free-day routines, transportation, and device coverage may dominate it.

**Resting heart rate.** Canli et al.'s 304 Turkish eighth graders had standardized seated RHR and earlier registry grades; total r=.099 was nonsignificant, without major socioeconomic controls ([2024, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/children11040396)). Xaipangna et al. found higher-achieving Lao secondary pupils had higher resting pulse, contrary to a lower-is-better assumption ([2015 original journal](https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JSST/article/view/44942); abstract-only). Tharion et al.'s 81 first-year medical students had RHR–later-marks rho=−.03, p=.80 ([2023, DOI](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.49087)). These populations, protocols, and analyses differ; pooling them into an RHR effect range would mislead. HRV, sleeping HR embedded in multivariate profiles, stress physiology, and accurate pulse measurement cannot substitute for an independently validated latest-RHR academic predictor.

## Deployed-window and interpretation boundary

The deployment definitions supplied for this review are: sleep uses yesterday's local noon to the earlier of now/today's noon, combining asleep intervals and including naps; steps, exercise, study, and recreational screens use the previous completed local day; RHR uses the latest finite positive reading within 72 elapsed hours; prior score is same-subject and 1–90 days old. **Every exact deployed window, and the frozen V2 model's prospective incremental performance, remains unvalidated by the external studies reviewed.** The timestamp, source/device, coverage, local-day boundary, and window definitions are provenance metadata, not additional academic features. This review proposes no model or feature changes.

Measurement is a separate question. One adult Apple sleep validation had only 20 successful Apple recordings, with average overestimation 19.6 minutes and agreement limits −38.35 to +77.55 minutes ([2025, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleepadvances/zpaf021)). A child Apple activity study's five-person free-living subsample showed 47.5% mean absolute percentage error for MVPA versus ActiGraph despite favorable laboratory results ([2021, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21196413)). A living Apple review found only three step studies under its criteria, using early generations ([2026, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-02238-1)). None validates every current device, adolescent population, or academic prediction. Measurement error is a possible limitation, not an established explanation for a model result.

Regionally, the contradictory Saudi screen finding is directly relevant but cross-sectional. UAE research in 158 female university students found no regularity/social-jetlag academic association; that is neither secondary-school evidence nor duration validation ([2025, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-90720-x)). Health recommendations for sleep or activity are not grade-maximizing thresholds. The searches found no strong UAE/GCC validation of these exact wearable inputs beyond prior grades; this is a scoped finding, not proof of absence.

Finally, the supplied completed NetHealth study—**207 students, 963 repeated terms—found essentially no incremental wearable value beyond prior score**. External associations do not overturn that result. Plausible confounding, noise, restricted variation, or population differences are hypotheses until tested. Shared-model sensitivity in WhatIf is not a personal causal response. Personal inputs and an RHR wellbeing baseline do not establish learned individual academic effects, and significance in a published regression is not evidence of better held-out forecasts.
